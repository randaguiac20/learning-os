# Discrete Math & Linear Algebra — hands-on

You have a real Linux machine on the right and a plain **CPU** — which is all you need. Every idea in this
lab runs in **milliseconds** with nothing but **numpy**: no GPU, no downloads.

In the next few minutes you'll **verify every claim in code** — the ritual is **predict → compute →
reconcile** (say the answer before numpy prints it):

- **Vectors** — the dot product as *similarity*, and the norm as length.
- **Matrices** — a matrix as a *transformation* of space, and `A·A⁻¹ = I` (the inverse undoes the verb).
- **Eigenvalues** — confirm `A·v = λ·v`, the direction a matrix only *stretches*.
- **The bridge** — a graph written as an **adjacency matrix**, with **matrix powers** counting walks and
  revealing reachable nodes.
- **Modular arithmetic** — `mod` (crypto's and hashing's engine) and the **pigeonhole principle** (why hash
  collisions provably exist).

> Tip: **type every command yourself** — and predict each result before you run it. Machine-checking a
> hand-calc is the habit (the gradient-check from M26, generalized): never trust a derivation you didn't
> independently verify.
