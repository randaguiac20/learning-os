# Calculus, Probability & Statistics — hands-on

You have a real Linux machine in the terminal on the right. In the next few minutes you'll touch the two
continuous languages under machine learning: **calculus** (how a model moves) and **probability &
statistics** (how you know it moved).

You will:

- compute a **numerical derivative** and watch it converge to the **analytic** one (the gradient-check habit);
- run **gradient descent from scratch** and see all three learning-rate regimes — converge, crawl, diverge;
- sample a distribution and watch the **mean converge to theory** (the law of large numbers);
- work the **Bayes mammogram** and be surprised by the base rate;
- put a **confidence interval** and a **p-value** on a model comparison — "is +2% real or noise?"

Everything runs in **Python with numpy** — no black-box optimizer, no statistics library. You'll write
results to files under `~/mathlab/`; two steps have a **Check** button that verifies real artifacts.

> Tip: **type every command yourself** — don't copy-paste. Deriving by hand *before* the code confirms it
> is the whole method (Learner's Law: bare hands first, tools verify).

Click **START** to begin.
